Before marble races were filmed and streamed, plenty of us built our own tracks with a spade and a bucket. You don't need a beach: any sandy slope works, a dune, a sandpit or a bank of building sand, and the longer the slope, the more fun. Here is how to build one, step by step, from the start at the top to the finish pit at the bottom.

::picture header

## 1. What you need

- **A small shovel or spade** to dig the channel.
- **A bucket** to carry water.
- **A watering can**, or just the bucket, to wet the sand.
- **Something flat to smooth with:** a ruler, a piece of wood, or your hand.
- **A few marbles**, one for each player.

::picture what-you-need

## 2. Pick the spot

Look for a sandy slope. The steeper it is, the faster the marbles go; the longer it is, the longer the race. **Damp sand holds its shape best**: dry sand crumbles and slides back into the channel, so build where the sand is a little wet, or wet it yourself as you go.

::picture pick-the-spot

## 3. Dig the channel

Start at the top and dig a groove down the slope. Make it **about the width of two marbles**, so marbles can pass each other but not get lost. Pile the sand you dig out along both edges to make **raised walls** on each side: they keep the marbles in the channel.

::picture dig

## 4. Water it

Pour water over the channel and the walls, so the sand sticks together. Don't flood it: wet it evenly, then **let it settle for a minute** before you go on.

::picture water

## 5. Flatten and smooth

Pat the floor and the walls flat and smooth with your ruler, piece of wood or hand. Every bump is a ramp: on a bumpy floor the marbles hop and jump out of the channel, on a smooth one they roll. Run a marble down to test it, and smooth again wherever it jumps.

::picture smooth

## 6. Build the start

At the top, build a **raised starting platform**: a flat patch of firm, wet sand where all the marbles can sit side by side. From it, make a **straight run-in** into the first part of the channel, so every marble gets a fair start and nobody is stuck behind. A stick laid across the front makes a starting gate: lift it, and they're off.

::picture start

## 7. Add bends and banking

A straight track is fast, but bends make it exciting. In a bend the marbles are pushed towards the outside, so **build the outside wall of each bend higher** than the inside one. Pack it firmly and wet it well: the outside wall takes every hit. If marbles still fly out, make the bend wider or the wall higher.

::picture banked-bend

## 8. Build a splitter

For the tricky part, split the channel in two round a mound of sand, so the marbles "choose" a way, then join the two channels again after the mound. The hard part is making it fair: **both channels must be equally fast**, so make them the same length, the same slope and equally smooth. If one side is shorter or steeper, the marble that takes it will almost always win. Race a few marbles and adjust until each side wins about as often.

::picture splitter

## 9. The goal

At the bottom, mark a **finish line** (a line in the sand or a stick), or dig a **finish pit**: a round hollow that catches the marbles as they arrive. With a pit, the first marble in is the winner.

::picture finish

## 10. Race!

Everyone picks a marble. Line them up on the start, lift the gate so they **all start together**, and the **first into the goal wins**. Then rebuild: make it longer, add more bends, and race again.

::picture race

## Stay safe

- **Marbles are not for children under 3.** They are small enough to swallow and can cause choking. In the US, marbles for children three and older must even carry a choking-hazard warning by law [1].
- **Dig only in sand, and never near the edge of the water.**
